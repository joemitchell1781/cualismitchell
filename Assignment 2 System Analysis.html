Evaluating the CUA Tower Website

Part 1: System Analysis Using Nielsen’s Heuristics

Inputs and Outputs

Input Output(s)
Clicking on the Magnifying Glass Appearance of the Search Box and the prompt to search the website
Hovering over the Menu Categories Display of the subcategories under that category (e.g. Campus and District under "News")
Clicking on the Archives Dropdown at the bottom of the page Displays a long scrollable list of every month of the Tower available on the page.
Clicking the Dateline of an Article Displays a page with all articles from the same month as that article, giving thumbnail images and the first several words of each article. There are navigation buttons at the bottom for cases where there are more articles than fit on one page.

Top 2 Strengths and Weaknesses

The top two strengths that I noted about this site are that the site has a clean, minimalist design and it has a consistent appearance for all articles, making it easy to learn how to engage with the site. The consistency works to make the site intelligible at all points and make it clear to the user what they can and can't do.
The top two weaknesses I noted were the very barebones search functionality and the lack of similarity to a real life newspaper. It's apparently not possible to see all articles from one issue, as the only way to see articles by date is to look for all of the articles from an entire month. The search feature has no clear indication of why the results are shown in the order that they are. They aren't chronological and they aren't able to be sorted or filtered in any way at all.

Part 2: Mini Usability Test

Comparing Test with Evaluation

The main similarities and differences I noted in the results of the evaluation I performed and the usability test were:

  • My volunteer focused more on the scrolling and tabbing through pages than the search function.
  • Both studies indicated poor navigation control was an issue, whether through the searching or the page number buttons on the website
  • While I found the tags on each article distracting personally, my volunteer saw them as a navigational aid.
  • The actual navigation was agreed upon as being intuitive and similar enough to other websites that it wasn't confusing to learn.

Final Recommendations

It's my recommendation that the Tower site undergo a thorough investigation of how to improve their search feature. Adding filters to the system and allowing for more involved searching will help with discovering specific articles. The Tower also should add further indicators of what the article has going on, perhaps with an indicator of where the article was originally published/if it appeared in print or only online.
